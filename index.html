How to enter a proof

The premises are already filled in. Add one line at a time until you reach the conclusion. On each new line, type a statement, then a justification: the line number(s) it comes from and the rule's abbreviation.

Example: to prove P • Q ∴ P, line 1 is the premise P • Q. On line 2 type P as the statement and 1 Simp as the justification. The line gets a ✓, and the proof is complete.

  • Justifications look like 1, 2 MP, 3 DM, ACP or 4-6 CP. Rule abbreviations are in the Rules reference below.
  • Press Enter to move from the statement to the justification, and again to add a new line.
  • A ✗ means the line doesn't follow yet; the message under it says why.
Insert: or type ~ & v > =, (x) for all, (Ex) for some

Welcome!

Pick a problem from the list. Each proof starts with the premises; add lines until you reach the conclusion. Every line needs a justification: the line numbers it uses and the rule, such as 1, 2 MP.

For a conditional proof, write ACP as the justification of the assumption. Close the subproof with a line justified like 3-5 CP. Indirect proof works the same way with AIP and IP.

Truth-table problems ask you to fill in a table and then classify what you found.
